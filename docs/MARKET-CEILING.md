# The market ceiling: where +1 is load-bearing, and what one more market costs

**Status: INVESTIGATION OUTCOME (#305), 2026-08-02.** An inventory and a costed
answer, not a rollout. The decision this feeds is `docs/DECISIONS.md` D115.
Nothing here changes behaviour.

## The short answer, for whoever is asked in a sales call

**We serve the United States and Canada. Nowhere else, and not with a
configuration change.**

23 sites assume a North American number: **2 XL, 8 L, 10 M, 3 S.** The two XL
ones are not features — they are the workspace's own `country` CHECK and the
NANP regex that every destination decision in the product reads through.

## What "another market" actually costs, and it is not a price

#305 asks for one additional market to be costed properly. The costing turned
up something better than a number.

**Telnyx sells UK and Australian numbers, and the local ones cannot text.**
Sampling 25 available numbers in each of GB, AU and IE returns feature sets of
`voice, emergency, local_calling, fax` — **zero SMS-capable**. Filtering
explicitly for `features[]=sms` does return results, and they are all
`phone_number_type: mobile`: `+447874…`, `+61468…`.

So internationalising is not "pay more per segment". It is **selling a mobile
number instead of a local business line** — a different product proposition
from the one on every marketing page, and one that collides with our own code:
`routes/porting.ts` already treats wireless numbers as a special case needing a
PIN and last-4.

Telnyx's published messaging pricing page lists **no GB or AU rates at all**,
so the per-segment cost could not be established from published data. That is
recorded as a gap rather than estimated, because a guessed rate in a cost model
is how `UNIT_COST_CENTS` stops being an upper bound.

## The trap for whoever does this, stated once

`runPreSendGates` (`apps/api/src/messaging/send.ts:153`) rejects any
destination `lookupAreaCode` cannot resolve, and only then branches on `US` and
`CA`. **Those two branches have no `else`.**

Today that is unreachable — the `!entry` throw above them catches every
non-NANP number, so nothing dispatches ungated. But the moment somebody widens
`lookupAreaCode` to a third country, a destination in it passes the resolver,
falls through both country gates, and dispatches with **no registration check
at all**. Carrier filtering of unregistered A2P returns no error: the message is
accepted, billed, marked sent, and never arrives.

**Widening the resolver and adding a country gate are one change, not two.**

## The cheap non-regrets

#305 asks these be separated from the speculative work, and the honest answer
is that **there are very few, because the codebase is already disciplined**:

- **E.164 is already stored raw.** `phone_e164` holds the full international
  form everywhere; nothing stores ten digits and re-adds a `+1`. The expensive
  version of this mistake was never made.
- **Compliance copy is already data-driven** where it matters: opt-out language
  lives in `packages/shared`, rejection guidance in a catalogue, and quiet
  hours resolve through the destination-clock ladder rather than a constant.
- **The one worth doing now is a comment, not a change** — the `else`-less
  country branch above. It costs nothing to write down and is the difference
  between a careful widening and a silent one. Done, in this document and in
  D115.

Everything else on the list is correctly deferred: doing it early means
maintaining a second numbering plan, a second registration lifecycle and a
second cost model for a customer who does not exist.

## What this is not

Not a plan, and not an ordering. Several entries are only solvable together —
widening the `country` CHECK without the send gate produces a workspace that
can sign up and cannot text, which is worse than refusing it at signup.
